Phase 2: AI Agent & Automation Built

Documentation

Reads a target web form's fields and matches them against your Phase 1 vault data — a zero-setup heuristic matcher by default, or a local Ollama model for better matches on oddly-labeled fields (nothing ever leaves your machine either way). A field only gets filled if the match is confident and your vault actually has data for it; otherwise it's left unmapped rather than guessed. The agent can type matched values into the form for your review, but it never clicks submit — that stays a manual step in your own browser. Form-filling itself lives on its own dedicated Fill a Form page — the Agent page is where the backend/hardware setup, remote model connections, and data permissions live.

Remote models (optional): you can also connect a ChatGPT (OpenAI), Grok (xAI), Gemini (Google), or Claude (Anthropic) account for tasks a local model handles less well — drafting a resume summary, a contract, or marketing copy. Local Ollama/LM Studio/heuristic matching is completely unaffected either way; nothing they process ever leaves this machine. A remote model only ever sees the categories of vault data you've explicitly checked in Data access permissions — unchecked by default, every one of them — and every real call is written to that account's usage log so you can check exactly what was actually sent, not just what was allowed.

AI providers (Steps 323-325): the Agent page's AI providers panel is the real answer to "what are my actual options for running a model on this site." Six starters ship tracked by default — OpenAI/ChatGPT, xAI/Grok, Google/Gemini, Anthropic/Claude, and the two local runners, Ollama and LM Studio — each with its own plans, pricing, strengths/weaknesses, and real, concrete setup instructions written specifically for implementing it as an EasyLifeAgent backend (not just generic "how to use this product" copy). A wider catalog of real, major providers — Meta, Mistral, Cohere, Amazon Bedrock, Azure AI Foundry, watsonx, DeepSeek, Alibaba Qwen, Perplexity, Together AI, Groq (fast-inference hosting — a completely different company from xAI's Grok, despite the near-identical name), Fireworks AI, Hugging Face, NVIDIA NIM, Replicate, and GPT4All — is one click away in the Add a provider dropdown; adding one tracks it and shows its card, removing one untracks it (never deletes its research history), and a provider still backing a connected account can't be removed until you disconnect it first. Only the six starters are actually wired as selectable backends today — every wider-catalog card says so plainly and points you to that company's own site instead of a fake Connect button. Pricing/plans data is real and cited: a background pass refreshes anything not checked in the last week (each card shows exactly when it was last verified and links to its real sources), a Refresh button forces an immediate re-check any time, and a failed check (a page changed, a site blocked the request) never blanks out or guesses over the last known-good numbers — it just says so.

Chat widget (Steps 193-195/328-330): Chat is no longer its own page — it's a persistent component on every page, collapsed to a small tab bottom-right or expanded to a full frame with one click, the same shape as a LinkedIn messaging drawer. Ask a real question (budget, documents, applications, profile facts all answered from real data, deterministic, no model required) or ask for something to be done — anything consequential (applying a field, deleting/importing a file, confirming an update) is only ever described with a link to the real page/button, chat itself never performs it. On a page with a real task registered (today, just Fill a Form's field-matching), the widget's header shows a real, live agent picker — which of the six wired AI providers can actually do this job, sourced from the same capability check Steps 311/312 already built — and switching there changes the one real global backend setting, the same one the Agent page's own setup panel shows. The widget's second tab, Fill a form from a file, picks an already-uploaded document (or uploads a new one) and uses its real extracted text as the fill source instead of your Profile — same hallucination guard as Phase 1's document intake (a field only fills if it was actually found in that document's text), same review-before-fill screen as a Profile-sourced run.

Tutorial

  1. Open the Agent page. The setup panel shows your detected hardware and a recommended local model tier.
  2. (Optional) Switch the backend to Local Ollama or Local LM Studio, enter/confirm a model, and click Pull model/Check connection — or just leave it on the default heuristic matcher, which works immediately.
  3. Scroll to AI providers — read a starter card's plans/pricing/strengths/weaknesses and expand its Setup instructions. Click Refresh on one and watch its "Last verified" line update to just now.
  4. Open Add a provider, pick one from the wider catalog (e.g. Mistral AI), and click Add — its card appears and a real pricing check kicks off automatically since it's brand new. Click Remove from this page to confirm it drops back out.
  5. Click the chat tab, bottom-right, on any page — it expands into a full frame. Pick a profile, click + New chat, and try "what does this month look like?" or "how many documents do I have?" — then try "delete my old documents" and notice it only describes what to do and links to the real page.
  6. Open Fill a Form and open the widget there — its header now shows the real agent picker for this specific task; try switching agents and confirm the Agent page's own setup panel reflects the same change.
  7. Under Fill a form, pick a profile and paste a target form's URL, then click Analyze form; review the matched fields (each shows what it matched to and the confidence) and click Confirm & Fill — nothing is submitted, that stays a manual step in your own browser.
  8. In the widget, switch to Fill a form from a file, upload a real document (or pick one already uploaded with extracted text), and confirm the review screen shows fields genuinely pulled from that document, not your Profile.
  9. Optional: from an AI providers card, click Connect account to add a ChatGPT/Grok/Gemini/Claude API key. Check the boxes you want to allow under Data access permissions, then select that provider as your backend — you'll see a one-time warning before saving.
  10. Click View usage log on a connected account any time to see exactly what's actually been sent to it.
